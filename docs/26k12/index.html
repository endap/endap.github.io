moving charge in perpendicular magnetic field

  • Sparisoma Viridi
5 Nov 2026 · 2 mins read

A charged point mass particle moving in perpendicular magnetic field is an intereseting phenomenon to discuss, since it can perform a circular motion with simple force, i.e. the magnetic force. Under influence of the force the partice will have constant speed and move in circle as long as the initial velocity is perpendicular to a constant magnetic field.

equations

Equation of motion for a point particle with mass $m$ and charge $q$ moving in constant magnetic field $\vec{B}$ can be written as

$$\tag{1} m \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt^2} + q \vec{B} \times \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = 0, $$

with initial velocity $\vec{v}(t_0) = \vec{v}_0$

$$\tag{2} \vec{B} \cdot \vec{v}_0 = 0 $$

to get the uniform circular motion.

functions

A circular path can be represented using parametric functions as folow

$$\tag{3} x(t) = x_c + R \cos (\omega t + \phi_0) $$

and

$$\tag{4} y(t) = y_c + R \sin (\omega t + \phi_0), $$

where center of the circle is $(x_c, y_c)$, circle radius is $R$, angular velocity of the circular motion is $\omega$, and initial phase of the motions is $\phi_0$.

kinematics

Since it is a uniform circular motion, where angular acceleration is zero, then the relation of linear velocity $v$ and angular velocity $\omega$ is simply

$$\tag{5} v = \omega R. $$

Following relations also hold between the linear motion variables

$$\tag{6} v_x(t) = \frac{dx(t)}{dt}, $$

$$\tag{7} v_y(t) = \frac{dy(t)}{dt}, $$

and

$$\tag{8} a_x(t) = \frac{dv_x(t)}{dt}, $$

$$\tag{9} a_y(t) = \frac{dv_y(t)}{dt}, $$

steps

  • Find $v_x(t)$ and $v_y(t)$ from Eqns (3) and (4) using Eqns (6) and (7).
  • Find $a_x(t)$ and $a_y(t)$ from previous result using Eqns (8) and (9).
  • Find arbitrary initial velocity $\vec{v}_0$ that obeys Eqn (2) if $\vec{B} = B_0 \hat{z}$.
  • Show graphically that Eqns (3) and (4) make the particle move in circle.
  • Show mathematically that Eqns (3) and (4) can form circle equation.
  • Show hat Eqns (3) and (4) are solution of Eqn (1) by subtituting the first two into the last one.
