magnetic force uniform circular motion

  • Sparisoma Viridi
4 Nov 2026 · 2 mins read

There is a gap in explaining uniform circular motion with Newton’s 2nd law of motion. In general any motion formulated using the law can have trajectory of the motion as the solution, if the equation of motion can be solved. In order to get the trajectory of uniform circular motion a certain force must be provided. The simplest form of the force is magnetic force acted on a charged particle moving with velocity always perpendicular to the force. If the magnetic force is constant and it moves in circle with constant speed.

equations

The differential equation representing equation of motion is simply

$$\tag{1} m \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt^2} + q \vec{B} \times \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = 0, $$

where

$$\tag{2} \vec{B} \cdot \vec{v}(t_0) = 0 $$

is the initial condition at $t = t_0$.

steps

  • Express a vector in its components, e.g. $\vec{p} = p_x \ \hat{x} + p_y \ \hat{y} + p_z \ \hat{z}$.
  • Formulate equation of motion for all direcitons, i.e. $x$, $y$, $z$.
  • Formulate also the initial conditions for direcitons, i.e. $x$, $y$, $z$.
  • Simplify the problem by choosing $t_0 = 0$.
  • Solve the equations simultaneously for the coupled terms between directions.
  • Show the parametric functions $x(t)$, $y(t)$, and $z(t)$.
  • Explain how the radius of circular path $R$ is related to
    • constant magnetic field $B$,
    • initial speed $v_0 = |\vec{t_0}|$,
    • particle mass $m$,
    • particle charge $q$.
  • Draw the trejectory using obtained parametric function.
  • Show that the trajectory indeed is a circle mathmematically using the parametric equations.
  • List the references used in solving the problem.
