midterm problem ideas

  • Sparisoma Viridi
9 Oct 2026 · 4 mins read · edit

I still have no idea type of problem to use in the midterm of two different courses. Open-ended and goal-less problems are then encountered, where the former has multiple possible solutions with no single correct answer, while in the latest the specific goal or desired outcome is not clearly defined 1. Both can be used and also stimulate creative thinking, even when LLM-based chatbots are still allowed to be used 2. A case in mathematics, assessment in the form of open-ended problem is used to identify students creative thinking, both in results and processes 3. On the other hand in the goal-less problem job of the students is to model the situation as best they can using the physics they know by saying which models apply and the reason, drawing the diagrams hat go with those models to represent the situation, and using the diagrams to analyze the situation 4.

An open problem in theoretical physics, a real problem and not just an exam, has been solved using AI-assisted discovery 5 and also in mathematics, which is the one of Millenium Prize Problems, a solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem 6, while in chemistry Chemical AI is evolving as part of AI-driven chemistry 7.

ideas

  • A chess-like board where each column has its on friction (the time required to pass through it), where as the whole there are many alternatives to travel from one corner to other opposite corner in diagonal. The goal is to find the fastest path 8.
  • Previous idea can be enhanced to have two matrices, friction coefficient matrix and elevation matrix, where the process of finding feasible paths will have at least three stages 9.
  • One-dimensional system of a fixed path with variation of velocity, acceleration to get minimum travel time with limited initial energy 10.

matrices

At least two matrices are required, which are

$$\tag{1} \mathbf{M}_f = \left( \begin{matrix} 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.2 \newline 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.2 & 0.2 \newline 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.2 & 0.2 & 0.3 \newline 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.2 & 0.3 & 0.4 \newline 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.1 & 0.2 & 0.3 \newline \end{matrix} \right) $$

and

$$\tag{2} \mathbf{M}_h = \left( \begin{matrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 2 \newline 1 & 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 \newline 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 & 3 \newline 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \newline 1 & 1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \newline \end{matrix} \right), $$

where the former is for friction, while the later is for elevation.

equations

Energy due to elevation is

$$\tag{3} \Delta E_h = mg(h_j - h_i) $$

while following is

$$\tag{4} \Delta E_f = - mgs\mu_k $$

due to friction, when agent move from cell $i$ to cell $j$.
