title and position

The title might be “Revisiting Three-Dimensional Cellular Automata as an Interpretable Framework for Understanding Barrier-Mediated Guided Bone Regeneration” which whould answer following question.

Can a simple 3D cellular automaton reveal latent descriptors of GBR progression that are currently inaccessible through routine clinical observation?

The proposed cellular automaton framework is not intended as a patient-specific predictive tool. Rather, it serves to identify mechanistically relevant parameters governing guided bone regeneration and to formulate hypotheses regarding measurable indicators of healing progression that may reduce dependence on repeated radiographic assessment.

Despite advances in radiographic assessment, guided bone regeneration remains a process that is observed only intermittently. Clinicians have limited access to the spatiotemporal evolution of tissue occupation within the defect, creating a need for conceptual frameworks that identify key parameters governing regeneration dynamics.

This work will not only to reduce radiographic exposure but more as to understand hidden healing dynamics and identifying surrogate indicators of regeneration progression.

minimal model

Minimal CA is effectively {B, S, Vr, Ve, M}.

  • Regenerable void (Vr) – space inside the defect compartment, beneath the membrane, that can be occupied by bone or soft tissue.
  • External void (Ve) – space above the mucosal surface or outside the defect, where no regeneration occurs.
  • Bone (B)
  • Soft tissue (S)
  • Membrane (M)

rules notation

A compact notation might be

​​GBR: R4(3) / R4(4) / P(3) / M

which reads naturallya as

Bone regenerates when at least four neighboring bone cells are present; soft tissue invades when at least four neighboring soft-tissue cells are present; bone persists; membrane acts as a barrier.

For a GBR paper, Regeneration / Preservation / Resorption may communicate the biology more clearly than the Game-of-Life terminology Birth / Survival / Death.

single-field ca

A single-field probabilistic CA can represent differential tissue invasion rates through transition probabilities. However, introducing a membrane state variable enables time-dependent modulation of these probabilities, thereby reproducing the transient barrier function characteristic of bioresorbable membranes.

Source of discussion is 7.

pca over ca

For membrane-free GBR simulations, a single-field probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) is adopted, where each lattice site stores only tissue identity ((E): empty, (B): bone, (S): soft tissue). Compared with a deterministic CA, the PCA better represents the biological competition between bone regeneration and soft tissue invasion by assigning soft tissue a higher occupation probability, reflecting its tendency to dominate defect filling in the absence of a barrier membrane.

Source of discussion is 8.

new cell types

The cellular automaton comprises six cell states: (X) (outside the computational domain), (E) (empty regenerative space), (B) (bone), (T) (soft tissue), (M) (membrane), and an optional additional state if future model extensions require it. The designation (E) is intentionally generic and denotes any biologically available space that can potentially be occupied by regenerating tissues, including defect cavities, extraction sockets, peri-implant gaps, and, in future guided bone regeneration (GBR) applications, open wound regions. This terminology is preferred over terms such as void, which may imply a permanently inaccessible or non-regenerative region and therefore becomes less appropriate when extending the model to scenarios involving wound healing and tissue ingrowth.

ca grid-resolution sensitivity

Investigating the influence of cell size in a CA model for GBR is worthwhile because changing the lattice resolution while maintaining the same physical defect size effectively alters the spatial discretization. A refinement study using grids such as 4×4, 8×8, and 16×16 can assess whether predictions depend on cell size, particularly regarding interface smoothness, curvature representation, lattice anisotropy, and healing behavior. Such an analysis can help identify a resolution that provides sufficient accuracy while limiting computational cost.

Source of discussion is 9.

time mapping

A single-field probabilistic cellular automaton (PCA) for Guided Bone Regeneration (GBR) represents the evolving tissue domain using a single lattice containing only tissue-type information, with state transitions governed by neighborhood-based probabilities. Because this formulation does not possess an inherent physical time scale, the correspondence between simulation iterations and physical time must be established through calibration using biologically meaningful transition rates or experimental healing data. In grid-resolution sensitivity analyses, the physical dimensions of the defect remain unchanged while the lattice resolution is varied, leading to changes in the physical size represented by each cell. To preserve comparable regeneration kinetics across different discretizations, the physical time associated with each iteration should decrease as the spatial resolution increases, and the transition probabilities should be adjusted accordingly. Calibration against experimental observations, such as defect bridging time, bone formation kinetics, or mineralized tissue evolution, provides a consistent framework for comparing simulations performed at different resolutions in terms of physical healing time rather than iteration count.
