mfl prototyping assessment

  • Sparisoma Viridi
1 Sep 2026 · 7 mins read · edit

In the process of prototyping, e.g. MFL (magnetic flux leackage) technology used in ILI (in-line inspection), a feature verification matrix is a useful tool, but not as the actual engineering assessment 1. A matrix structure is recommended 2. The scope itself is more to MFL sensing and technology only instead of whole pig 3, with some discussable deliverables 4 as part of final report 5, that must also include state of the art of MFL technology 6. Following are items to be used in creating the assessment matrices.

technology aspects

Assessment aspect of MFL vendors based on the underlying technologies. Several aspects—magnetization, sensors, configuration, and data processing—are considered as separate categories for classifying MFL technologies, forming a matrix that is hopefully independent. Magnetization includes field orientation, distribution, excitation, and source. Sensors cover inductive, Hall-effect, magnetoresistive, magneto-impedance, magnetic-core, magneto-optical, and quantum magnetic sensors. Configuration considers dimensionality (1-, 2-, or 3-axis), arrangement (linear, planar, etc.), and sensor density/spatial pitch 7.

magnetization

There are suggested four categories of MFL inspection techniques based on magnetization 6, which are later simplified as follows 8.

  • Field orientation
    • Axial / longitudinal
    • Transverse / circumferential
    • Oblique / spiral / helical
    • Multi-directional / rotating
  • Field distribution
    • Uniform / quasi-uniform
    • Localized / concentrated
    • Gradient / non-uniform
  • Field excitation
    • DC / quasi-static
    • Pulsed
    • AC / low-frequency AC
  • Field source
    • Permanent magnet
    • Electromagnet
    • Hybrid magnet

sensor

Based on type of magnetic sensor there might be about seven categories as follows 9.

  • Inductive sensors: Search coil, pickup coil
  • Hall-effect sensors: Hall sensor, Hall IC
  • Magnetoresistive (MR) sensors: AMR, GMR, TMR
  • Magneto-impedance (MI) sensors: GMI, MI
  • Magnetic-core sensors: Fluxgate, micro-fluxgate
  • Magneto-optical sensors: Faraday-effect, Kerr-effect, magneto-optical indicator
  • Quantum magnetic sensors: SQUID, OPM, NV-center sensors

configuration

Based on sensor configuration three categories can be summerized as follows 10.

  • Dimensionality
    • Single-axis
    • Dual-axis
    • Triaxial
  • Arrangement
    • Linear
    • Planar
    • Circumferential
    • 2D array
    • 3D / spatial array
  • Sensor density / spatial pitch
    • Sparse
    • Dense
    • Ultra-dense

data processing

MFL data processing can be classified into following categories 11.

  • Signal Processing: Filtering, denoising, normalization, Fourier transform, wavelet transform, peak detection.
  • Physics-Based Methods: Analytical models, FEM, model-based inversion, magnetic circuit models.
  • Statistical Methods: Regression, PCA, discriminant analysis, clustering, probabilistic models.
  • Machine Learning: SVM, random forest, decision tree, k-NN, gradient boosting.
  • Deep Learning: CNN, RNN/LSTM, autoencoder, transformer.
  • Physics-Informed Learning: PINN, physics-constrained NN, hybrid physics–ML models.

performance and implementation criteria

In addition to classifying vendors according to their underlying MFL technologies, the assessment considers practical performance and implementation characteristics. These include sensor quality and performance, data acquisition and storage capability, and material/mechanical durability. These criteria are evaluated using operational data, experimental results, technical specifications, and other vendor-provided evidence, where available 12.

sensor quality and performance

Sensor quality and performance are fundamental to the effectiveness and reliability of MFL technology. The assessment should focus on the sensor’s ability to detect, characterize, and consistently measure metal-loss defects under expected pipeline operating conditions. The following criteria are ranked by their importance in differentiating vendors and ensuring reliable inspection performance 13.

Table 1. Sensor quality and performance when ranked by importance.

RankCriteriaWhat to assess
1Detection sensitivityAbility to reliably detect small and critical metal-loss defects.
2Measurement accuracy & repeatabilityAccuracy of defect sizing and consistency between inspections.
3Sensor coverageEffective inspection coverage with minimal blind spots.
4Signal-to-noise ratioClarity and stability of MFL signals for reliable interpretation.
5Defect discriminationAbility to distinguish metal loss from welds, geometry changes, deposits, and other features.
6Lift-off toleranceAbility to maintain performance despite changes in sensor-to-pipe distance.
7Data quality & resolutionCompleteness and resolution of data for analysis and reporting.
8Operating speed rangeAbility to maintain detection performance across required inspection speeds.
9Sensor durabilityResistance to wear, vibration, temperature, and harsh pipeline conditions.

Recommended scoring emphasis is to put the greatest weight on 1–5, since these have the strongest impact on actual MFL inspection performance and vendor differentiation.

data acquisition and storage

This section assesses the vendor’s capability to reliably acquire, record, preserve, and manage MFL inspection data throughout the inspection lifecycle. The criteria focus on data quality, completeness, traceability, storage capacity, and accessibility, ensuring that acquired data is sufficient for accurate defect assessment, validation, re-analysis, and long-term integrity management 14.

Data acquisition & storage must focus on whether the MFL system captures sufficiently high-quality, complete, traceable data and preserves it for re-analysis. ISO 22500:2026 specifically covers MFL testing and evaluation of corrosion/mechanical damage, while industry guidance emphasizes onboard acquisition, data-loss assessment, and retention of raw data.

Table 2. Data acquisition and stotrage when ranked by importance.

RankAssessment aspectWhat to evaluate / vendor item
1Acquisition quality & resolutionSensor density, sampling rate, ADC resolution, dynamic range, and ability to capture small/critical defects without signal loss.
2Data completeness & integrityContinuous recording, detection of missing/corrupted channels, automatic data-loss monitoring, and clear run acceptance criteria.
3Raw-data retentionAvailability of full raw MFL signals, not only processed indications; sufficient onboard capacity for the complete inspection.
4Data synchronization & traceabilityAccurate correlation of MFL signals with distance/odometer, tool speed, clock/time, GPS/markers and pipe identifiers.
5Storage robustness & capacityRugged, non-volatile storage suitable for shock/vibration, with adequate capacity and reliable data transfer after the run.
6Data format & accessibilityOpen/exportable formats, ability to re-process historical data, metadata retention, and compatibility with integrity-management systems.
7Data security & backupSecure transfer, checksum/error detection, backup capability, audit trail and controlled access to inspection data.

Suggested weighting for vendor scoring is as follows.

Acquisition quality 25% + data integrity/completeness 20% + raw-data retention 15% + synchronization/traceability 15% + storage 10% + accessibility 10% + security/backup 5%.

This weighting puts the emphasis on the factors that most directly affect MFL detection reliability and the ability to defend/re-analyse inspection results. MFL systems can generate very large datasets, making acquisition rate, storage capacity and efficient data handling particularly important.

material durability

Material durability should focus on whether the MFL inspection system can reliably operate in the field over repeated runs and under harsh pipeline conditions 15.

Table 3. Material durability when ranked by importance.

RankMaterial durability aspectKey vendor assessment criterion
1Mechanical integrityWithstands pressure, shock, vibration, bending, welds, valves, and other pipeline features without damage.
2Sensor & electronics robustnessReliable under temperature, pressure, vibration, shock, and repeated inspection cycles.
3Wear & abrasion resistanceInspection surfaces, wheels, skids, and sensor carriers maintain performance despite continuous pipe-wall contact.
4Corrosion & chemical resistanceMaterials remain reliable in crude, gas, water, H₂S/CO₂, and other pipeline environments.
5Battery / power-system durabilityPower system reliably supports the full inspection duration under expected operating conditions.
6Component service life & maintainabilityLong replacement intervals, low refurbishment requirements, and good spare-parts availability.
7Proven field durabilityDemonstrated performance through comparable pipeline runs, low failure rates, and documented service history.

For vendor scoring, the first 4 criteria should carry the highest weight, as they most directly affect inspection reliability and data quality.
