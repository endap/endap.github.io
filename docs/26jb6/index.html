diff-eqn and trajectory

  • Sparisoma Viridi
29 Oct 2026 · 2 mins read · edit

Solving a differential equation representing a motion can produce system trajectory. Some examples are given in this post.

parabolic motion

Trajectory of a parabolic motion can be obtained by solving following differential equation

$$\tag{1} m \frac{d^2 \vec{r}}{dt^2} - \vec{w} = 0, $$

where $\vec{w} = m \vec{g}$ is gravitational force near earth surface.

harmonic motion

Sinusiodal trajectory is solution of harmonic motion with following diferential equation

$$\tag{2} m \frac{d^2 \vec{r}}{dt^2} - k\vec{r} = 0, $$

where usually it simply that $\vec{r} = x \hat{x}$ or $\vec{r} = y \hat{y}$.

circular motion

To obtain solution in the form of a circular motion, the differential equation should consider vector cross product operation as follows

$$\tag{3} m \frac{d^2 \vec{r}}{dt^2} + q\vec{B} \times \frac{d \vec{r}}{dt} = 0, $$

where the case is a charge $q$ moves in plane perpendicular to external magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

tasks

  • Find the case where Eqn (1) can give linear motion instead of parabolic motion.
  • Show that Eqn (2) can only give harmonic motion in 1-d if there is not any initial velocity in the direction other than the direction of restoring force. Otherwise it will give sort of circular motion with oscilating radial position.
  • Show that Eqn (3) can only give circular motion if $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{B} = 0$. Otherwise it will give helical motion.
