coffee-bean-drying n-stages

  • Sparisoma Viridi
21 Aug 2026 · 4 mins read · edit

In a discussion group 1 some unconfirmed observations trigger the idea of n-stages for coffee bean drying process, i.e. sun drying followed by fan drying 2. Even it sounds simple, in locations with limited natural airflow, using a fan to direct airflow onto a damp area is an effective way to speed up the drying process 3, while sun drying is one of the oldest and most widely practised methods of preserving fruits and vegetables 4. It is the mucilage to be consider as a factor in drying process.

model

A warm discussion transforms the initial 3-compartement model to a simply 2-compartemen model with the constants are function of mode (sun or wind) and the existence of mucilage 6

Figure 1. Model 1 with three compartments (left) and model 2 with two compartments (right).

Illustrations the two models are given in Figure 2, where the firts model is avoided since measurement can not give the value of second compartment (mucilage).

Then the last the model is

$$\tag{1} \frac{dC}{dt} = -kC $$

with $C(t)$ represents measurable total moisture of the bean + attached mucilage. The constants depends on drying mode ($m = 1$ for sun only, $m = 2$ for wind only, and $m = 3$ for sun-wind), which defined as follows

$$\tag{2} k = \left\{ \begin{matrix} k_{\rm sun}, & m = 1, \newline k_{\rm wind}, & m = 2, \newline k_{\rm sun-wind}, & m = 3, \end{matrix} \right. $$

where the last constant is

$$\tag{3} k_{\rm sun-win} = \left\{ \begin{matrix} k_{\rm sun}, & 0 \le t \lt t_s, \newline k_{\rm wind}, & t > t_s, \end{matrix} \right. $$

that depends on switching time $t_s$ when the sun mode change to win mode. Experiments in mode 1 and and 2 will give $k_{\rm sun}$ and $k_{\rm wind}$, respectively, while mode 3 confirm the value of $C$.
